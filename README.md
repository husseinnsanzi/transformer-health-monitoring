## Problem Statement

Distribution transformers are critical assets in electrical power distribution networks. Their failure can lead to electricity interruptions, equipment replacement costs, emergency repairs, and additional financial burdens for electricity utilities.

In Rwanda, the management of distribution transformer failures raises important questions about asset protection, maintenance costs, and the financial risks associated with unexpected equipment damage. For Rwanda Energy Group (REG) and other electricity stakeholders, the costs associated with transformer failures and insurance arrangements are worth investigating as part of a broader effort to improve distribution network reliability.

Although conventional protection devices help safeguard transformers against certain electrical faults, they do not eliminate every cause of failure. Factors such as prolonged overloading, thermal stress, insulation deterioration, and developing internal faults may require additional condition monitoring and maintenance strategies.

This project investigates whether machine learning can complement conventional transformer protection and maintenance practices by identifying abnormal operating patterns and classifying potential faults at an earlier stage.

The long-term motivation is to explore whether better condition assessment and more informed maintenance planning could help reduce preventable transformer damage, avoid some unnecessary maintenance expenses, improve equipment reliability, and potentially reduce financial exposure associated with transformer failures and insurance claims.

The project will initially use suitable public datasets and, where justified, clearly identified synthetic data. Any conclusions concerning REG's actual transformer failure rates, insurance expenditure, existing protection systems, or potential cost savings will require reliable evidence and, where appropriate, authorized operational data.
